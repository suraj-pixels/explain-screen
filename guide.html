Explain 1Screen

Every design decision, ringed on the live screen where it sits, with the reason, what was considered instead, what it costs, and the source it rests on. Your team sees the design and its argument together, so nobody has to ask why something is there.

  • Works on the real screen, not a copy
  • One decision per note, one line to read
  • Every reason links to its source
  • Any project: prototype, app, or live site
  • Against what’s in production, down to the atom

  6. 6

  8. 8

Anatomy of a decision

One note answers the questions a review asks

Only the decision is required. Every other field answers one question someone will ask in the room, so it can be answered before it is asked.

dWhat did we decide?

One line, stated as a choice. “Status is a dot and a word”, not “This is the status”. Always there.

wWhy?

The user’s task, and the principle it rests on.

altWhy not the obvious other way?

What was considered and dropped. It answers the question every review asks.

bWhat does it cost?

The honest downside. A choice with no cost usually wasn’t a choice.

relWhat does it touch?

Other parts it depends on, drawn as dashed lines on the screen.

pWhat does it rest on?

NN/g, Laws of UX, WCAG 2.2, ARIA, Material, Fluent, HIG: 89 sources, every link checked.

refWho else does it?

A real product that works the same way, named only when verified.

fix · wasWhat now, what before?

The fix for an issue, or what a change replaced.

setupShow me

Puts the screen in the note’s state: a drawer open, an error showing, a step of a flow.

Seven levels

From one word on the screen to the whole product

Writing notes means walking the screen at every level and asking that level’s questions. Whatever survives the question becomes a note, and whatever doesn’t survive gets questioned: does it need to exist?

element

Why this word, size, weight, colour, icon, position? What does it look like in every state?

amounts right-aligned in tabular figures

group

Why are these together, what holds them together, and why this order?

the whole row is one button

component

Why this control and not the usual alternative? What is its default, and why?

segmented control over a drop-down

section

What job does it do? Why here, why this size, what does it push away?

search, then status, then sort

screen

What is the one thing to do here? What is the reading order? What was left out?

no KPI tiles: one summary line

step

What does the user see and do now? What can go wrong, and how do they get back?

approve, then Undo, instead of a confirm

system

Does it hold everywhere: one meaning per colour, one name per thing, one format per fact?

indigo only ever means “act”

Verdicts

Every note says how settled it is

Settled what the design is

  • KeepSound, and it stays.
  • KeptChecked again, unchanged from the last version.
  • ExistingThe live product already does this: the baseline of a redesign.
  • NewAdded in this version.
  • ChangedDifferent from before, and the note says what it was.

Open collected into Findings

  • IssueA problem, always with its fix.
  • Open questionThe team decides.
  • ProposalAn idea beyond the brief.

Findings stay findings until someone picks. The Findings topic gathers them from every topic and copies them as Markdown, with a link to each one on the screen.

Against the baseline the same notes, read against what’s in production

  • RemovedGone from the screen. It can’t be ringed, so it’s listed with what replaced it.
  • ChangedStill there, done differently. The note says what production had.
  • MovedThe same thing in a new place, like a date that moved to the right edge.
  • AddedNew to the product. It often needs data the backend doesn’t keep yet.
  • SameKept from production on purpose, so people don’t relearn it.

How a designer uses it

Showcase, decide, review

The same notes do three jobs. What changes is what you write first and what you take away.

Showcase

Presenting a design to your team, a PM or a client.

In the room
  1. Open Explain on the screen itself.
  2. Step the topics with →: the screen, its sections, then a flow.
  3. Flow steps set the screen up themselves, so nothing is clicked by hand.
You get
“Why is this here?” is answered before anyone asks. A link like ?xp=4.2 opens straight on a decision for the deck or the chat.

Decide

Two versions, or a choice still open.

In the room
  1. Each version has its own notes under the same topics.
  2. Compare with swaps the version and stays on the topic.
  3. Every choice lists its alternative, and every change says what it replaced.
You get
A decision per open question, with the reasons written down. The losing version is kept, with why it lost.

Review

Checking a screen: yours, a teammate’s, or what’s live.

In the room
  1. Reviewers press + Note, click the part and type the finding.
  2. It is ringed straight away and kept as a draft.
  3. Findings gathers every issue, question and proposal.
You get
Copy as Markdown for the review doc or ticket, and Copy as code to make the drafts permanent notes.

For experts

Four lenses on the same notes

A redesign has to answer more than “why this?” What is different from what people use today? Why is this 15 pixels and not 16? Does the new flow cost fewer clicks than the old one? Which decisions still have no alternative written down? Each lens reads the notes you already have and answers one of these.

What’s different from what we have?

Against production

A switch under the panel’s title reads every note against the live product instead of the last iteration. A topic lists the whole difference, part by part, with screenshots of production.

  • Removed, changed, moved, added, same
  • What production had, in one line per part
  • The data each new part needs from the backend

Reads basebvbwdata

Why is it this big, this far apart, this colour?

Inspect, down to the atom

Hover any part of the real screen and click to pin it. The panel names where it sits in the whole and what it is made of, measures it, and judges every value by the design system.

  • Screen › section › component › element
  • Size, padding, gap, radius, type, colour, token
  • Contrast and target size, against WCAG
  • Pin one part, hover another: the gap between them

Reads anatomyrules and every note’s selector

Is the new way actually shorter?

Flows, step for step

A flow topic shows the same task as it is done in production beside the new steps, each marked by what the person does: a click, typing, a modal, a scroll, a read.

  • Counted: clicks, typing, modals, then steps
  • The note under it says what the old way couldn’t do

Reads before on the topic, k on each step

What haven’t we justified yet?

A heatmap of which reasons the notes give at each level, from element to system. The screen audit counts every text style, gap and colour in use and flags whatever the system doesn’t cover.

  • The decision log, for a doc or a spreadsheet

The named parts one level down: the atoms of a molecule, the molecules of a section.

The system rule it follows, or a warning when nothing in the system covers it.

Every note written on this part, from any topic, with its verdict.

Pin one part, hover another: red lines with the distance. A black label means it’s off the scale.

The system, written once

Nobody writes “15 px because…” two hundred times. The type scale, the spacing scale, what each colour means and the radii go into rules once. Inspect and the audit then answer every size, gap and colour question from it, and the gaps in the system show up on their own.

// in the entry, or in EXPLAIN_CONFIG for every screen
rules: {
  type:   { '24': 'Title, one per screen',
            '15/600': 'Names: who is asking, read first',
            '14': 'Body and purpose', '12': 'Labels' },
  space:  { v: [4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32], why: 'The 4-point scale' },
  color:  { '--act': 'Indigo = act', '--warn': 'Amber = needs a look',
            '--bad': 'Red = blocked', '--ok': 'Green = ready' },
  radius: { '8': 'Controls and panels' }
},
anatomy: [['.row:not(.th)', 'Claim row', 'group'], ['.row .who b', 'Name', 'element'], …],
root: 'main'   // what the audit reads

How a value is judged, from the demo:

  • 15 / 600✓ on the scale: “Names: who is asking, read first”
  • padding 6⚠ not on the spacing scale, in 18 places: add 6 to the system, or move them to 4 or 8.
  • padding 10⚠ not on the spacing scale. Click the row and the audit rings all 11 places it’s used.
  • #e08a14✓ a meaning: “Amber = needs a look”, from the --warn-dot token
  • #40407a⚠ saturated, no meaning set: the avatar’s initials, a colour that says nothing yet.

Add it to any project

Three kinds of host, one engine

The engine is never edited per project. A project gives it three things: which screen is showing, how to reach a state, and room for the panel.

Two tags and a notes file

  • One screen per page: nothing else to set.
  • Several screens: give each entry a match on the hash.
  • Versions: other on each entry and show(id) in the config.
<!-- the page -->
<link rel="stylesheet" href="explain.css">
<script src="approvals.xp.js"></script>
<script src="explain.js"></script>

// approvals.xp.js
EXPLAIN.approvals = { name: 'Approvals · v2', topics: [
  { id: 'row', name: 'A claim row', setup: () => DEMO.reset(), notes: [
    { s: '.row .st', t: 'A dot and a word', v: 'change', lv: 'element',
      d: 'The word carries the meaning.', p: ['w_color'] }
  ]}
]};

Dev builds only

  • Tag each screen root: <main data-explain="checkout">, or match the route.
  • Router changes are picked up on their own.
  • Setups use the app’s dev hooks, or click the real UI with XP.go and XP.click.
// main.ts (Vite): React, Vue, Svelte …
if (import.meta.env.DEV) {
  import('./explain/explain.css');
  import('./explain/checkout.xp.js')
    .then(() => import('./explain/explain.js'));
}

// the app decides which screen it is
window.EXPLAIN_CONFIG = {
  key: auto => store.modal ? 'refund-dialog' : auto()
};

Injected into your signed-in window

  • Staging, QA, production, or a competitor’s site.
  • Only your tab gets it. Nothing is sent anywhere, and closing the tab removes it.
  • Setups only navigate and click. They never save, send or delete shared data.
# opens a tab in the real-window-verify window
node .auth/xp.mjs open https://qa.example.com/orders \
  --inject .auth/explain.css,.auth/explain.js,.auth/orders.xp.js \
  --cdp 9224 --xp 1

# prove every ring lands, on any host
node xp.mjs check demo/index.html

What makes it different

Made to travel, and built for reviews

Most annotation tools are wired to one project. This engine runs anywhere: a prototype, an app in code, or a live site, configured rather than forked.

Any host

Config in place of edits: which screen, how to switch versions, colour, label, push or float. It follows hash routes, pushState routers and screen roots on its own.

Live apps

xp.mjs open injects Explain into staging or production in your signed-in window, without touching the app.

+ Note

Reviewers pick any part of the screen and write a finding. It gets a selector, a ring and a draft, which copies out as code.

Findings

Every issue, question and proposal, grouped and exported as Markdown with links, ready for a ticket.

Deep links

?xp=4.2 opens topic 4, note 2, with the screen set up. Every note has a copy-link button.

Your sources

Add the project’s design system or research to the source list, and your own verdicts and levels.

Keyboard and phone

Notes are reachable with Tab and Enter. On narrow windows the panel becomes a bottom sheet, so the rings stay visible.

Four more sources

WCAG 2.5.2, 2.2.1 and 2.4.11, and the ARIA modal dialog pattern: the gaps real reviews run into.

Against production

A redesign reads against what’s live, with the removed parts listed, not only against its last iteration.

Inspect and audit

Any part measured and judged by the design system, and the whole screen audited against it.
